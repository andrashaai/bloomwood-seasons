Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-17 · Effective date: 2026-10-17 · Applies to Bloomwood Seasons for iPhone and iPad, version 1.0 and later

Bloomwood Seasons is a quiet little game, and we'd like to keep it that way. This page explains in plain words what the game does and doesn't do with your information.

The short version: the game has no account and no servers of our own. It uses Google Firebase to see anonymous usage and crash data, so we can improve the game and fix bugs. We don't track you, we don't use that data for ads, and we don't sell it. Your garden is stored on your device. Purchases are handled by Apple.

1. Who we are

Bloomwood Seasons is made by an independent developer, called “we” or “us” on this page. For the purposes of data protection law (such as the EU and UK GDPR), the Bloomwood Seasons developer is responsible for any personal data handled as described here. You can reach us through the channel in Contact.

2. What we collect

The game has no account or login, and we never ask for your name, email address or phone number. We don't run servers for the game. The game does send some anonymous technical data to Google through the Firebase tools described in section 6. In short:

  • how the game is used: for example levels reached, orders completed, garden areas restored, tutorial steps, spending of in-game currency and purchases of in-game products;
  • basic device and app information: device model, iOS version, app version, language and time zone;
  • a random ID of this installation of the app (not linked to you or your Apple Account);
  • crash logs and diagnostics when the app crashes.

The game does not collect:

  • your name, email address, phone number or contacts;
  • your precise location (the game has no location permission and never reads GPS). Google may work out a rough country or region from your internet connection's IP address;
  • advertising identifiers (IDFA) or any other identifier that follows you across apps;
  • photos, files, health data or anything from other apps.

3. What stays on your device

To keep your game going, the app saves a few things in its private storage on your iPhone or iPad:

  • your garden, board, items, currencies, level and collection;
  • your settings (music, sound effects, haptics, reduce motion);
  • the home location you picked and when you last changed it;
  • which purchases have been delivered, so they aren't given twice.

Your garden itself is not uploaded. We can't see it, and we don't keep a copy. (The anonymous usage events in section 6 describe what happens in the game, but they are not a copy of your save.) The app is not synced through iCloud. Like other app data, it can be part of your own device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup on your computer), depending on your iOS settings. Apple handles those backups under the Apple Privacy Policy, and we can't access them.

The app also writes short technical messages to your device's own system log. They stay on your device.

4. Your home location

Your garden follows the season of a home that you choose from a list built into the game. The game doesn't use GPS, doesn't ask for location permission and doesn't know where you really are. Its first suggestion comes from the region setting of your device (for example “United Kingdom”). That happens on the device. The home you choose is not sent to us. You can change your home at any time in the game's Settings.

5. Purchases

Optional in-app purchases (gem packs and the Starter Pack) are processed entirely by Apple through the App Store. We never receive your payment details, name, email address or Apple Account. To deliver an item, Apple's StoreKit gives the app a signed transaction record on your device. The app checks it there and doesn't send it to us. Apple gives developers sales reports that don't identify individual buyers. Apple's handling of your purchase is covered by the Apple Privacy Policy.

6. Analytics and crash reports (Google Firebase)

The game includes three tools from Google Firebase: Analytics (usage events), Crashlytics (crash reports) and Remote Config (a setting we can change remotely).

  • What they collect: the usage events, device and app information, installation ID and crash data listed in section 2. Analytics also sees purchase events (product and price) that come from the App Store. Remote Config looks at your country, language and app version to pick the right setting.
  • Why: to understand where players get stuck and improve the game, to find and fix crashes, and to retire broken versions. If we find a version that is broken, Remote Config can show an “Update” screen that sends you to the App Store. The game works fully offline and the screen only appears if we set a minimum version.
  • What it is not: not sold, not used for advertising or tracking, not combined with data from other companies' apps. The game has no user ID, does not use your advertising identifier, does not link the data to your Apple Account and doesn't ask for App Tracking Transparency permission. Advertising personalization features are turned off.
  • Who processes it: Google acts as our processor under the Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms. See the Google Privacy Policy and the Firebase privacy information. Data is sent over an encrypted connection and may be processed in the United States and other countries where Google operates.
  • How long: Analytics event data is kept as configured, currently Google's default (2 months). Crash reports are kept by Crashlytics for about 90 days.
  • Debug builds used while developing the game don't send this data.
  • Opting out: the game has no in-app switch for this. You can ask us to delete the data tied to your installation, see section 12. Deleting the app also stops all collection, and Apple's own analytics sharing is separate (section 7).

7. Crash reports from Apple

If you've turned on Share With App Developers in iOS (Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements), Apple may give us crash reports and basic app statistics. These come from Apple and are separate from Firebase, and they don't identify you. We use them only to find and fix bugs. You can turn this off at any time in the same place.

8. Ads

The current version shows no ads. No ad network or ad software is included and no advertising identifier is used. That's why the game doesn't ask for permission to track you.

A future version may work with an advertising partner (for example AppLovin MAX). If that happens, we will update this policy before that version is released. The update will name the partner, say what data it collects and why, and explain how to opt out, and the game will ask for your permission where the law or Apple requires it (for example through Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt). We'll update the App Store privacy details at the same time.

9. Postcards and sharing

If you send a garden postcard, the game draws the image on your device and hands it to the standard iOS share sheet. You choose where it goes, if anywhere. We don't host, receive or see the image.

10. Third parties and tracking

  • The companies involved in running the game are Apple (App Store, purchases, optional crash reports and your own device backups) and Google (Firebase, section 6).
  • We don't sell personal information, and we don't share it for advertising. Firebase data is used only to run analytics and crash reporting for us. We also don't track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • If you contact us through GitHub, GitHub stores your message under the GitHub Privacy Statement.

11. Children

Bloomwood Seasons is a general-audience game that is suitable for all ages. It isn't made specifically for children, and it isn't in the App Store's Kids category. The game collects no name, email, account or precise location from anyone, and it does not use data for advertising or tracking, including for children under 13 (US) or under the age of digital consent in your country (13 to 16 in the EU and UK). The anonymous Firebase data in section 6 is collected the same way for every player. Parents can stop or approve in-app purchases with Apple's Screen Time and Ask to Buy features. If you believe a child has sent us personal information through a support request, contact us and we will delete it.

12. Keeping and deleting data

  • Game data is on your device and stays there until you remove it. To delete it, delete the app (press and hold the app icon → Remove App → Delete App). This erases your progress for good. Copies in your own device backups are kept or removed according to your backup settings.
  • Purchases are recorded by Apple in your Apple Account purchase history, under Apple's policies.
  • Support requests stay on GitHub until they are no longer needed. Then we close them, and we delete them on request.

Firebase data (section 6) is not linked to a name or account, so we can only find it if you give us the installation ID. We can't read that ID from the outside, so deletion requests for it are handled on a best-effort basis: email us and we will explain how to find it and delete the matching data in Firebase. Analytics data also expires by itself after the retention period above.

We don't hold an account or profile about you, so there is nothing else to delete on our side.

13. Your rights

EU and UK (GDPR and UK GDPR)

You have the right to ask for access to your personal data, and to have it corrected, deleted, restricted or given to you in a portable format. You can also object to processing, and you can withdraw any consent you gave. The Firebase data in section 6 is not tied to your name or account, so we can usually only act on it if you can help us identify your installation. You are welcome to ask anyway. We process the Firebase data in section 6 on the basis of our legitimate interest in keeping the game stable and improving it (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); you can object to this at any time by contacting us. If you send us a support request, we use what you send to answer you, also on the basis of our legitimate interest, and to fix a problem you reported. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority: in the EU, the authority in your country (list of EU authorities); in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office.

California (CCPA/CPRA) and other US states

In the last 12 months we collected, through Firebase, anonymous usage data, device and app information, an installation ID, approximate location derived from the IP address and crash data (internet or other electronic activity and identifiers). We did not sell it or share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we don't use or disclose sensitive personal information. The only exception is what you choose to send in a support request (for example your GitHub username and your message), which we use only to reply. You have the right to know what we hold, to delete it, to correct it, and to opt out of sale or sharing. There is no sale or sharing to opt out of; Google acts as our service provider. We will never treat you differently for using these rights. Residents of other US states with similar privacy laws have the same choices.

How to exercise your rights

Contact us as described below. Please don't post personal details in a public request. Just tell us that you'd like to make a privacy request, and we'll arrange a private way to continue. We answer within one month (GDPR) or 45 days (CCPA). We don't need to verify your identity for requests about data we don't have.

14. Changes to this policy

If the game changes in a way that affects your privacy (for example when an ad partner is added or we start collecting something new), we'll update this page and the effective date above before that version reaches you. If a change means the game would collect personal data, the game will tell you in the app and ask for consent where that is required.
